# THE MEMETRIX II: LIQUIDATION
*A Meme Royale parody, episode 2. Target length 2:40 (16:9, letterboxed 2.2:1). Original script; only genre tropes are borrowed.*

**Logline:** Anon has learned not to sell. Now the market is learning to sell *him*. Agent Jeanphil has been upgraded:
he copies himself into every wallet in the Memetrix. To stop the crash, Anon must find the one character who holds the
keys, out-fight an army of Jeanphils, and face the Whale who has dumped on every "chosen one" before him.

**Why it's bigger than episode 1:** a real antagonist plan (the rug), a ticking clock (the liquidation bar), three new
characters, a fight Anon *wins* (payoff for losing the training fight), a 12-clone brawl, a flight, a villain monologue,
an impossible choice, and a cliffhanger.

## 1. THE RUG (0:14-0:38)

**INT. MOM'S BASEMENT - NIGHT.** Same set, now *lived in*: pizza boxes stacked higher, a second CRT, a whiteboard with a
hand-drawn chart. MENSA at the CRT, lit green. MICHI pacing. SLERF in the gaming chair. ANON, standing taller than in
episode 1.

**MENSA** *(not turning round)*
It's not a dip. It's a rug. Somebody's pulling the floor out from under the whole Memetrix.

**MICHI**
Jeanphil?

**MENSA** *(spins the chair; the CRT behind him shows one Jeanphil face, then four, then sixteen)*
Jeanphil. He's not chasing us anymore. He's *copying* himself. Every wallet he touches becomes him.

**ANON**
Then we find who's paying him.

**SLERF** *(slowly)*
The Whale... has... the exit.

*Beat. Everyone waits. Slerf says nothing more.*

**ANON**
...Where is the exit, Slerf?

**SLERF** *(suddenly fast, panicked)*
Nobody-knows-the-only-one-who-knows-is-the-Keyholder-and-the-Keyholder-is-at-the-café!

*They all stare at him.*

**MOM** *(V.O., through the ceiling)*
Honey, are you going out? It's very red outside!

## 2. THE BURNING CAFÉ (0:38-1:08)

**INT. CAFÉ "FINE" - CONTINUOUS.** **New set.** A small diner where everything is quietly on fire: flames on the tables,
the counter, the menu board. Nobody reacts. Warm orange light against the cold green of the Memetrix outside the window.
THIS-IS-FINE sits at the counter with a coffee. TOSHI, small, sits beside him clutching a glowing key ring of seed words.
SWORDCAT stands at the back, blade sheathed.

**THIS-IS-FINE** *(sipping)*
Sit. Have a coffee. Everything is fine.

**ANON**
Your café is on fire.

**THIS-IS-FINE**
It's been on fire since 2021. You get used to it.
*(nods at Toshi)* You want the Keyholder. Everyone wants the Keyholder. She opens any door in the Memetrix.

**TOSHI** *(tiny voice)*
Twelve words. I never say them out loud.

**THIS-IS-FINE**
Swordcat decides who walks out with her.

*SWORDCAT draws. The blade catches the firelight.*

**THE TEST (fight, 0:52-1:04).** This is the payoff for episode 1, where Anon got pushed over in two seconds. Choreographed
with contact, at striking distance, every blow landing on a body:
1. Swordcat lunges; Anon **blocks** with his paw (the block Slerf used on him).
2. Second slash; Anon leans out of it in a short slow-motion beat (0.3x), the blade passing a whisker from the bag.
3. Swordcat spins; Anon **catches the blade between his paws.** Freeze.

**SWORDCAT** *(lowers the blade, the first words she says)*
He holds.

**TOSHI** *(hops down, takes Anon's paw)*
Then I go with him.

**THIS-IS-FINE** *(as the ceiling behind him collapses in flames)*
This is fine.

**THE WHALE**

**ANON**

**THE WHALE** *(the monitors light up one by one: five other bags, five other cats, all holding)*
Every cycle, the market makes someone who will not sell. Every cycle, I sell to him.

**ANON**

**THE WHALE**
Because this time you brought friends.

**ANON** *(walks toward the right door)*
I don't sell.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

### New builds
- **Sets:** the burning café (fire via emission + flame cards + flicker light, an orange key against green exterior), the
  white room (seamless white cyclorama, a 6 x 8 monitor wall built with the `screen()` helper and per-monitor stills),
  rain for the rooftop (streak particles, wet floor roughness and puddle decals).
- **New effects:** green shockwave (expanding emissive ring + displacement), rocket candle with a sparks trail, the
  liquidation bar (2D in the finish, tracked to Michi), the "price impact" crack on the title.
- **New voices:** whale (deep, slow British), swordcat (two words only), this-is-fine (calm, warm), toshi (small, quiet),
  Jeanphil x12 (layered with delays, like the triplets).
- **Music:** the game's battle music for the brawl; near-silence plus a low drone in the white room.

### Applied from episode 1's fix list (non-negotiable)
1. **Measure, never guess:** eye heights and face depth on front + side grids for every new character before any glasses,
   masks or props are fitted.
2. **Props on bodies:** glue to arm-weighted skin vertices on the held pose (`posed`/`fist_top`/`place`); for poses the
   game clips don't have, build a pose from clips (idle body + mirrored arm, as for Slerf's candle presentation) or use an
   IK arm *with a pole* (as for Farter's phone). Never force arms with plain IK: it twists them into the body.
3. **Hand-offs:** match the two props' heights to within 3 cm, approach from the side so bodies don't overlap, and check
   the closest distance between the two meshes.
4. **Cameras:** the solver rejects cameras inside characters; use `with=[...]` for two-shots and `cam_at` for inserts
   (placed on the measured target, with a ray test for a clear view). No over-the-shoulder shots past bulky characters.
5. **Fights:** striking distance measured, blocks and contact on bodies, the struck character moves with the blow, the
   fallen character lies on the floor (check for floating and tail clipping).
6. **Projectiles:** fired from the posed hand, staggered, timed to arrive mid-slow-motion, clearance checked against the
   bent body, the orbit camera never crossing their path.
7. **Pronunciation:** check every unusual word in Kokoro's phonemes (the "leverage" fix) and use `PH:` pieces.
8. **Review:** check frames for every shot (fog off for speed), before/after sheets for the user, full-film review at
   3 frames a second plus the pop detector before sending. **The real render keeps the fog, lights and motion blur.**
9. **Disk:** delete old scene builds as you go; the render preflight stops below 1 GB free.
